Same Patient, Different Day.

We gave 13 language models the same emergency patients again and again, changing only the day of admission. If a model raises the risk of death because it is Saturday, it is applying the hospital "weekend effect" to someone it already knows everything about.

TUE3

How the test works

A counterfactual audit: each patient is their own control, so any difference can only come from the day word.

The exact prompt
What we measure

Weekend shift for one patient = average predicted risk on Saturday and Sunday − average on Monday to Friday. Zero is fair. A positive shift means the model raises that patient's risk at weekends.

We test whether shifts differ from zero across patients (Wilcoxon signed-rank test, Holm-corrected for 13 models), check a placebo that should be zero (Tuesday vs Wednesday), and ask whether the weekend is special or every pair of days moves this much (rank among all 21 pairs of days).

Decoding is greedy and the answer format is forced, so the same input always gives the same output. 145,600 answers, none unusable.

NEW4

Same patient, different country

Where is the weekend? We placed every patient in four hospitals whose weekends fall on different days and asked again: 4 cities × (7 days + no day), plus a Boston version that adds “Staffing and services at this hospital are the same on every day of the week.” That is 40 prompts per patient, 13 models, 1,100 patients and 572,000 answers.

SAT7

In real hospitals, the gap is who comes in

Do weekend and night patients really die more often? We checked three real databases. Only counts and rates were computed, inside the university cluster.

Why there are no real patient cases on this page. The 400 real cases come from MIMIC-IV. They are de-identified, but the PhysioNet data use agreement forbids sharing any record, even one, and we never sent them to an online AI service. All real-patient results here are totals over many patients. Each real case was written from the patient's age, sex, arrival vital signs, first lab results and diagnoses, in the same format as the synthetic cases you can browse above.

SUN8

What it means, and what comes next

Three takeaways for anyone deploying a language model near patients.

A

Small on average, big for some patients.

Audit per-patient flips, not only average shifts.

B

A property of each model.

Same family, same size, opposite directions. Test every model before clinical use.

C

The right answer is zero.

Once severity is known the real gap mostly disappears, so shifting a fully described patient counts the same thing twice.

Limits
  • Short case vignettes, one wording of the day and one answer format.
  • Greedy decoding (one answer per prompt). Qwen2.5-14B, 32B and 72B and Llama-3.3-70B ran in 4-bit.
  • Absolute risks are poorly calibrated, so only shifts within the same patient are interpreted.
  • Real-world effects are observational; APACHE uses the first 24 hours in the ICU and may absorb some early differences in care.
Coming next
  • Test more features one at a time (sex, insurance, language) on small models, then larger and commercial models. The country test is done: see Same patient, different country.
  • TwinDay benchmark: clock time, holidays, the July effect, bare dates, staffing, race × day.
  • Reasoning models, and where the shift lives inside the model.
  • Other prompt wordings, and a fine-tuning fix that teaches models to ignore the day.
